1. Zebra crossings — the best cheap win, and better now than a week ago. Roads today are flat coloured triangles with no markings whatsoever. A crossing is a short barred rectangle laid across the carriageway at a known point, and the machinery already exists: the level-finish gate is exactly this, a painted decal over the road. You'd need the bearing to lay the bars square, which streets.nearest already answers. And it matters more since you dropped Blaha to 20 m — street markings are invisible from a hundred metres and unmissable from twenty.

2. Real building footprints — the biggest possible win, and a big job. Right now buildings are invented: the generator walks the block edges and places frontages. Józsefváros's signature is the closed courtyard block, and an invented frontage can't produce one — it produces a plausible European street that could be anywhere. 7509 real footprints would produce Józsefváros. But that displaces the generator and everything hanging off it: collision boxes, the garden/bare-block logic, landmark reservation, the whole "does this house sit on a railway" pass. Days, not hours.

3. Storey counts — real heights instead of a random 12–24 m. Cheap if you're already re-fetching, and from the air the height texture is much of a city's fingerprint. I was still counting these when the server started timing out, so I don't have the number.

4. Street trees (808) — the tree rig exists and trees currently only appear in green areas. This is placement data, nothing new to build. It would change how the streets feel more than the number suggests.

Not worth it: traffic signals. 390 of them, invisible at any altitude you fly at.

If you want one, I'd take the crossings — smallest change, and it lands where you've just put the player.
